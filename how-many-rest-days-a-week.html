How many rest days a week do you need?

Why rest days count, and how to place them · 30 October 2026

Short answer: most people do well with one to three rest days a week. If you go to the gym two or three times a week, you already have them. If you go five or six times, plan at least one full rest day and avoid training the same muscles hard on consecutive days.

Why rest days matter

Training is the signal; recovery is when you actually adapt. Muscles repair and get stronger in the days after a session, not during it. Without enough rest, sessions start to feel heavier, sleep can suffer, and small aches turn into reasons to stop.

There’s a habit reason too. A plan with rest built in is one you can keep for years. A plan that needs you every day is one bad week from collapse.

How many rest days, by schedule

43Use an upper/lower split so back-to-back days hit different muscles.
5–61–2Needs a split and sensible intensity. Keep at least one full rest day.

The usual advice is to leave about 48 hours before training the same muscle group hard again. That’s why splits exist: legs on Monday, upper body on Tuesday, and neither is training through the other’s recovery.

Signs you need more rest

  • Your usual weights feel heavier for more than a session or two.
  • Soreness hasn’t faded by the next time you train that area.
  • You’re sleeping badly or feel flat for days.
  • You’re dreading sessions you used to look forward to.

Any of these is a reason to take an extra day, not to push through. Pain that’s sharp or doesn’t go away is a reason to speak to a GP or physio.

What to do on a rest day

Rest doesn’t have to mean the sofa, though it can. Walking, an easy cycle, a swim or some stretching all help you feel better without getting in the way of recovery. They also count towards the NHS’s 150 minutes of weekly activity.

A rest day isn’t a missed day

This is where daily streaks get it wrong. They treat the day you’re supposed to rest as a failure, which nudges people to train when they shouldn’t, or to feel bad when they don’t. Counting your week instead (“did I hit three this week?”) puts rest back where it belongs: in the plan.

Questions

How many rest days a week do I need?

Most people do well with one to three. If you train two or three times a week you already have enough. If you train five or six times, keep at least one full rest day.

Is it OK to go to the gym every day?

It can be if you vary the muscles you train and keep intensity sensible, but most people get better results and stick with it longer with at least one rest day a week.

How long should you rest between training the same muscle?

The usual advice is about 48 hours before training the same muscle group hard again.

What should I do on a rest day?

Anything easy: walking, a gentle cycle or swim, stretching, or genuinely resting. Light activity can help you recover and counts towards your weekly activity.

How often should you go to the gym?

A weekly gym schedule you can keep · 29 October 2026

Short answer: two or three times a week is enough for most people, and the right number is the one you can keep in a busy week. A modest plan you hit every week does more than an ambitious one you abandon in February.

What the guidance says

The NHS physical activity guidelines for adults ask for two things each week:

  • Strengthening activities that work all the major muscle groups on at least two days.
  • At least 150 minutes of moderate activity, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, spread across the week.

Two gym sessions a week covers the first. Walking, cycling to work or a third session covers the second. Nothing in the guidance asks for five days a week.

Pick your number by your worst week, not your best

Most people set a target from their most motivated moment, usually a Monday in January. Then a late meeting, a cold or a weekend away knocks out a session, the week counts as a failure, and the habit goes with it.

Set the target from an ordinary, slightly-too-busy week instead. If you think you could manage four, plan three. Going more than you planned feels good. Going less than you planned feels like a fail, even when you still went twice.

Simple weekly gym schedules

These are schedules for when to go, not what to do once you’re there. Any sensible full-body or split programme fits inside them.

Two days a week

MonTueWedThuFriSatSun
Gym––Gym–––

Good for a first habit, or a demanding job. Leave two or three days between sessions so a missed day can slide without wrecking the week.

Three days a week

MonTueWedThuFriSatSun
Gym–Gym–Gym––

The classic. A rest day between each session, and the weekend free as a back-up if one slips.

Four days a week

MonTueWedThuFriSatSun
GymGym–Gym–Gym–

For people who already go regularly and want more. Usually paired with an upper/lower split so back-to-back days work different muscles.

Make the days specific

“Three times a week” is easy to postpone until it’s Friday. “Monday, Wednesday, Friday, after work” isn’t. Decide the days and a rough time once, so you’re not deciding every morning.

Keep one day as a spare. If Wednesday goes wrong, Saturday picks it up and the week still counts.

Count weeks, not days

A daily streak breaks on the first rest day, which is the day you’re supposed to rest. Counting weeks fixes that: the question is just “did I hit my number this week?” One bad week then costs one week, not the whole streak.

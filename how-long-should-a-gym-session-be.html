How long should a gym session be?

And why a short session still counts · 30 October 2026

Short answer: 45 to 60 minutes is plenty for most people, and 20 to 30 minutes is a perfectly good session on a busy day. Past about an hour, most people are chatting, scrolling or resting longer, not training harder.

What fits in an hour

  • 5–10 minutes warming up.
  • 30–45 minutes on four to six exercises, a few sets each, with sensible rests.
  • 5 minutes cooling down or stretching, if you like to.

Rest between sets is what stretches sessions out. Heavy compound lifts need two or three minutes; most other exercises need a minute or so. Supersets (alternating two exercises that use different muscles) are the easiest way to save time.

The 30-minute session

When the day’s gone wrong, a short session beats no session. Pick two or three big movements (a squat or leg press, a push and a pull), do a few hard sets of each, and leave. You’ll have done most of what mattered.

Short sessions also protect the habit. The cue (it’s Wednesday, I go to the gym) gets repeated either way, and repetition is what makes it automatic. More on that in how long a habit takes to form.

How it adds up over a week

The NHS guidance is strength work on at least two days, plus 150 minutes of moderate activity across the week. Three 45-minute sessions and some walking get you there comfortably. You don’t need long sessions; you need regular ones. See how often to go.

When longer makes sense

Longer sessions are fine if you enjoy them, train for a specific sport, or combine weights with a long cardio block. Just don’t let the idea that a “proper” session is 90 minutes stop you from going when you only have 30.

Questions

Is 30 minutes at the gym enough?

Yes. Two or three big exercises with a few hard sets each covers most of what matters, and keeps the habit going on a busy day.

Is a 2-hour gym session too long?

For most people, yes. Beyond about an hour, extra time tends to go on longer rests rather than more useful training.

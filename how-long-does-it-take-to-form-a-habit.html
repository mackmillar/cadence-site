How long does it take to form a habit?

What the research says, and what it means for the gym · 30 October 2026

Short answer: about two months on average, with a very wide range, and exercise tends to sit at the slow end. The “21 days” figure you’ve probably heard isn’t from a study of habits at all.

Where “21 days” came from

The number traces back to a 1960 self-help book by a plastic surgeon, Maxwell Maltz, who noticed patients took “a minimum of about 21 days” to get used to a change in their appearance. Over the years “a minimum of about” fell away, and it became a rule it was never meant to be.

What the research found

The study most people cite is Lally and colleagues at UCL (2009). They asked 96 people to pick a daily behaviour and repeat it in the same situation for 12 weeks, rating each day how automatic it felt.

  • The median time to reach a plateau of automaticity was 66 days.
  • The range ran from 18 to 254 days, depending on the person and the behaviour.
  • Exercise habits took longer to become automatic than simpler ones, like drinking a glass of water with lunch.
  • Missing a single day didn’t meaningfully set people back.

A later study using check-in data from tens of thousands of gym members reached a similar conclusion: going to the gym takes months, not weeks, to become habitual.

What that means if you’re trying to make the gym a habit

Expect it to feel effortful for a while

If week three still takes willpower, nothing has gone wrong. That’s the normal part of the curve. Most people quit around here because they assume it should be easy by now.

A missed session doesn’t reset the clock

The UCL finding that one missed day didn’t matter is the most useful line in the paper. Habits are built by the overall pattern, not an unbroken chain. Treat a missed session as weather, not a verdict.

Keep the cue the same

Habits form around a context: the same days, roughly the same time, the same gym. “Monday, Wednesday, Friday after work” becomes automatic far faster than “three times a week, whenever”. If you’re still choosing, read how often to go and when to go.

Make the bar low enough to clear on a bad day

Turning up is the habit. A short session on a tired evening still repeats the cue and the routine. See how long a session needs to be.

Count weeks, not days

A daily streak breaks on your first rest day. A weekly one asks a fairer question: did you hit your number this week? That keeps the pattern visible through the slow middle months, which is exactly when it’s needed.

Questions

How long does it take to form a habit?

In the best-known study, from UCL, the median was 66 days, with a range from 18 to 254 days depending on the person and the behaviour. The popular 21-day figure isn't based on habit research.

How long does it take to make going to the gym a habit?

Usually months rather than weeks. Exercise habits take longer to become automatic than simpler daily habits, so expect it to feel effortful for the first couple of months.

Does missing a day break a habit?

No. In the UCL study, missing a single day didn't meaningfully affect how automatic the habit became. The overall pattern matters more than an unbroken streak.

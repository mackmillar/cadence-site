Gym anxiety: how to start going when you feel out of place

Practical ways through your first few weeks · 30 October 2026

Feeling nervous about the gym is extremely common, and it fades with familiarity faster than almost anything else. Almost everyone there is thinking about their own session, not yours. The goal for the first few weeks isn’t a great workout. It’s to make the place feel ordinary.

Why it happens

Gym anxiety (sometimes called “gymtimidation”) usually comes from a few specific worries: not knowing how the equipment works, feeling watched, not knowing the unwritten rules, or feeling like you don’t look the part. Each one has a practical fix.

Before your first visit

Go at a quiet time

Mid-morning and early afternoon on weekdays, or the last hour before closing, are usually calm. Avoid weekday evenings from 5pm to 8pm and January, at least at first. When gyms are quietest has more.

Book the induction

Most gyms offer a free induction when you join. Someone shows you round, explains the machines and answers questions. It turns the unknown into somewhere you’ve already been.

Write a short plan

Three or four exercises on your phone, with sets and reps. Knowing what you’re doing next removes most of the hovering, and the hovering is what feels conspicuous.

Your first few sessions

  • Start with machines. They have instructions on the side and guide the movement for you.
  • Keep it short. Twenty or thirty minutes is a successful first visit. See how long a session should be.
  • Wear headphones. They help you stay in your own head and signal you’re busy.
  • Learn the basic etiquette. Wipe down equipment, put weights back, and ask “how many sets have you got left?” if you want to share. That’s most of it.
  • Go back soon. The second visit is far easier than the first, and the fifth is easier still. Familiarity is the cure.

How to start going to the gym and keep going

Pick a small, fixed plan: two days a week, the same days, the same time. Small and fixed beats ambitious and vague, especially when nerves are part of it. How often to go has a few simple schedules.

If anxiety is getting in the way of more than the gym, it’s worth talking to your GP. The NHS pages on anxiety are a good place to start.

Questions

How do I get over gym anxiety?

Make the gym familiar: go at a quiet time, book the free induction, take a short written plan, start with machines and keep first visits short. It eases quickly with repeat visits.

What should I do on my first time at the gym?

Keep it simple: a short warm-up, three or four machine exercises from a written plan, and 20 to 30 minutes in total. The aim is to leave feeling it was manageable.

When is the gym quietest for beginners?

Usually mid-morning and early afternoon on weekdays, and the last hour or two before closing.
